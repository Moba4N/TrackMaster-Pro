TrackMaster Pro

User Manual · Version 1.5.0
Track planning for model railway layouts in all common gauges – with height planning, helix and timber list, modules, levels and benchwork, scenery, room planning, wiring and signals, plan check, costs and 3D view. For Windows, Android and the browser – with a step-by-step introduction for everyone planning for the first time.

Contents

    1Welcome

    TrackMaster Pro is a planning program for model railway layouts. You set up the baseboard, lay track from the catalogues of the common track systems, plan gradients and helixes, design the scenery, wire the layout and look at the result in 3D – before you buy the first piece of rail.

    What TrackMaster Pro can do

    • Track plans in all common gauges: Z, N, TT, H0, H0m, H0e, 0, gauge 1 and G, with many track systems and about 900 track pieces – straights, curves, turnouts, crossings, double slips, buffer stops and flexible track.
    • Baseboards in many shapes: rectangle, L, U, T, Z, E and G shape, or a ring with an open centre – each leg with its own dimensions – or a layout made of modules that can be split, attached, moved and rotated.
    • Levels: up to 10 levels with their own base height, each one can be shown or hidden; the terrain follows.
    • Benchwork: a timber frame with legs including material and cutting lists; rib construction and roadbeds for open-grid benchwork.
    • Free-form flexible track: start at a track end and drag; the track bends along and snaps onto the target.
    • Height planning: gradients over whole track sequences, round or oval helixes, single or double track.
    • Timber list for the helix: all cut pieces with edge allowance and saw allowance, supports, fixings and sheet requirement.
    • Scenery: hills and mountains, water, buildings, trees and tunnels.
    • Wiring and digital: feeders, isolating joints, power sections, reverse loop detection, light signals with prototypical aspects, detectors, decoder addresses, routes and a wiring plan.
    • Room: the room with walls, doors, windows, radiators and furniture around the layout – the check reports aisles that are too narrow and blocked doors.
    • Background image: put a floor plan, sketch or old track plan under the plan and scale it exactly with a dimension line.
    • Help with laying track: parallel track at a fixed spacing, height profile as a chart, locking, grouping and aligning tracks, usable length of sidings.
    • Plan check: track spacing, clearance heights, baseboard edge, gradients, scenery, room, usable lengths, open track ends and checks for long vehicles.
    • 3D view: the layout from every side and from below, with benchwork and embankments, a cab ride, a running train and light according to the time of day.
    • Your way of working: view rotatable to any angle, five designs, seven fonts in four sizes, German or English interface.
    • Results PRO saving large plans (more than 50 tracks), parts list with prices and your own stock, helix timber list, rib construction, roadbeds, printing and exports (SVG, PNG, DXF).

    2Installation

    Windows

    Requirements

    • Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit
    • about 400 MB of free disk space
    • screen of at least 1280 × 720 pixels, 1920 × 1080 recommended
    • for the 3D view, a graphics card with DirectX support (present in every current PC)

    No additional runtime is needed – everything TrackMaster Pro requires is included in the installation package.

    Installing

    1. Double-click the file TrackMasterPro-1.5.0.msi.
    2. If Windows SmartScreen reports “Windows protected your PC”: click More info and then Run anyway.
    3. Follow the setup wizard: accept the licence terms and choose the destination folder (default C:\Program Files\TrackMaster Pro).
    4. Start the installation. Windows asks for permission to make changes – confirm with Yes.
    5. After a few seconds the installation is complete. Close the wizard.

    You will then find TrackMaster Pro in the Start menu and as a shortcut on the desktop. The manual is also in the Start menu.

    Updating and uninstalling

    Simply install a new version over the existing one: the installer detects the older version and replaces it. Your plans and your licence are kept. To remove the program, open Settings › Apps › Installed apps, select TrackMaster Pro and click Uninstall. Your plan files and the licence file are not deleted.

    Silent installation for administrators

    The package can be deployed to several computers without prompts:

    CommandEffect
    msiexec /i TrackMasterPro-1.5.0.msi /qninstallation without user interface
    msiexec /i TrackMasterPro-1.5.0.msi INSTALLFOLDER="D:\Programs\TrackMaster Pro" /qninstallation into another folder
    msiexec /x TrackMasterPro-1.5.0.msi /qnuninstallation without user interface

    Android

    Requirements

    • smartphone or tablet with Android 8.0 or later; a tablet of 10 inches or more is particularly pleasant for planning
    • about 100 MB of free storage

    Installing

    1. Copy the file TrackMasterPro-1.5.0.apk to the device – via USB cable, cloud storage or as an e-mail attachment.
    2. Tap the file on the device, for example in the Files app in the Downloads folder.
    3. The first time, Android asks whether this app may install unknown apps. Tap Settings, allow it and go back.
    4. Confirm with Install. TrackMaster Pro then appears in the app overview.

    Install an update the same way over the existing app; your current plan is kept.

    New versions

    At start-up the program briefly checks the download page for a new version and reports it in the status bar. Under File › Updates… you can check yourself at any time and open the download page with installer, app and manual if you wish. Nothing is ever installed automatically; without an internet connection the check is simply skipped.

    In the browser (web version)

    TrackMaster Pro also runs without installation in a current browser (Edge, Chrome, Firefox or Safari), on a PC as well as on a tablet. The web version has the same tabs and features as the Windows program. On first use it loads a few megabytes and starts after a few seconds; after that it comes from the browser cache.

    • Plans: the current plan is saved continuously in the browser and is there again on the next visit. File › Save downloads it as a .mpr file. File › Save as… (Ctrl+Shift+S) asks for the file name; in Chrome and Edge you also choose the folder, and Save then writes straight into that file. File › Open loads a .mpr or .trackmaster.json file. The content is the same as on Windows and Android: there you open a .mpr file by choosing “All files” in the Open dialog.
    • Licence: under File › Licence… choose the licence file or paste its content. It applies only in this browser; after the site data has been deleted it has to be installed again.
    • 3D view: needs WebGL. If the browser reports that it cannot show 3D graphics, switch on hardware acceleration.
    • Layout suggestion: Only in the web version, Tracks › Layout suggestion… builds small layouts from your track stock (see Layout suggestion in chapter 8).
    • Printing and exports are downloaded as files or output via the browser's print dialog.

    If the web version is embedded in a website, the Home button at the top left of the title bar leads back to that website's home page.

    3Licence and activation

    The Pro edition is activated with a personal licence file. The file is digitally signed and cannot be altered; the program checks it at every start, without an internet connection.

    Installing the licence

    1. Save the licence file you received (extension .lic) on the computer, for example in the Downloads folder.
    2. Open File › Licence… or Home › Licence.
    3. Click Install licence… and select the file.
    4. The dialog now shows Pro, the name of the licensee and, if applicable, the expiry date. The indicator at the bottom left of the status bar changes from “Basic” to “Pro”.

    The licence is stored in the user data (%APPDATA%\TrackMaster Pro\trackmaster.lic) and applies from now on at every start. On Android you install the licence the same way via File › Licence; you choose the file from the device storage.

    Online activation (up to two devices)

    A licence that is not bound to a computer may run on two devices at the same time – for example on the computer at home and on a tablet, on Windows, Android or in the browser. On the first start with the licence the device registers once over the internet; after that it checks about once a week that the registration still exists. The licence dialog shows the status in the Activation section.

    • Without internet Pro keeps running for up to 14 days after installing the licence. A device that has been activated once stays activated without internet.
    • If both places are in use, a third device stays on the basic edition. The message names the devices in use.
    • To change devices, choose Release this device in the licence dialog on the old device. Then click Activate now on the new device. If the old device is no longer available, the publisher releases the place.
    • In the browser every browser counts as a device of its own. If the site data is deleted, it registers as a new device.

    Computer-bound licence

    A licence can be bound to a particular computer or device. For this the publisher needs the machine ID (on Android: device ID). It is shown in the licence dialog; with Copy you put it on the clipboard and can paste it into an e-mail.

    When a Pro feature is chosen

    In the Basic edition, a note appears when you save plans with more than 50 tracks, and for the parts list, timber list, printing and the exports. With Licence… you open the licence dialog directly. Your plan stays open unchanged.

    CautionIn the Basic edition, plans with more than 50 tracks cannot be saved. When you close the program, start a new plan or open another one, TrackMaster Pro asks whether the changes should be discarded.

    4First steps for beginners

    Have you never planned a model railway on a computer before? Then this chapter is for you. It explains the most important terms, how to use the mouse and keyboard, and leads you step by step to your first small layout. You need no previous knowledge and no licence – everything works in the Basic edition.

    The most important terms

    A track plan is a drawing of your layout seen from above, at the correct scale. It is put together from individual track pieces – just like the real layout is built from the track you buy. You will meet these words all the time:

    TermWhat it means
    GaugeThe size of the model railway, for example H0 (scale 1:87, the most common), N (1:160, smaller) or Z (1:220, very small). You choose the gauge at the start; it determines which tracks are available.
    Track systemA track range from one manufacturer. Pieces of one system fit together exactly. Choose the system you want to buy or already own.
    Track pieceA single part: straight, curve (curved track), turnout (where the track divides), crossing or buffer stop (the end of a siding).
    Track endEvery track piece has ends where the next one connects – a straight has two, a turnout three. An end without a connection is called an open track end and is marked orange in the plan.
    RadiusHow tight a curve is, measured from the centre of the circle to the middle of the track. Small radii save space; large ones look better and long coaches run more reliably.
    BaseboardThe surface the layout is built on – often a wooden board, for example 2 × 1 m.
    MillimetresAll dimensions in the program are in millimetres: 1 m = 1000 mm, 1 cm = 10 mm. A board of 2 × 1 m is therefore 2000 × 1000 mm.

    More terms are explained in the Glossary at the end of the manual.

    Mouse and keyboard – what you need

    ActionHow to do it
    ClickPress the left mouse button once briefly. This selects tracks and presses buttons.
    DragHold down the left mouse button, move the mouse, then let go. This is how you move tracks.
    Move the viewHold down the right mouse button and move the mouse – the plan moves with it.
    Zoom in and outTurn the mouse wheel. The 0 (zero) key shows the whole layout again.
    UndoCtrl+Z – hold down Ctrl and press Z – takes back the last step. As often as you like.
    CancelEsc (top left on the keyboard) ends a tool or closes a dialog without changes.
    DeleteClick a track and press Del.
    Don't be afraid of mistakesYou cannot break anything. Every step can be undone with Ctrl+Z, and Home › History… even jumps back 20 steps at once. Feel free to try everything.

    Where do I find what?

    All commands are in the ribbon at the top. It is divided into tabs: Home, Tracks, Scenery, Room, Wiring, View and 3D. Clicking a tab shows its buttons. On the far left is the File button with New, Open, Save and Print. When the manual says Tracks › Gradient…, it means: click the Tracks tab, then the Gradient… button.

    On the left is the track library with all track pieces, in the middle the drawing area with your plan and on the right the properties: they show everything about what you have just clicked. Move the mouse over a button and wait a moment – a small note (tooltip) explains what it does. More about the window is in chapter 5.

    Your first layout in 10 steps

    We build a simple oval with a siding on a 2 × 1 m board in H0 gauge. It takes about a quarter of an hour.

    1. Create a new plan. Click File and then New (or press Ctrl+N). The New plan window opens.
    2. Choose gauge and baseboard. Under Gauge choose H0, as the shape Rectangle. Enter 2000 as the width and 1000 as the depth (millimetres!). Click Create plan. An empty baseboard now lies on the drawing area.
    3. Choose the track system. At the top of the track library on the left, choose the track system. The list below now shows all track pieces with number and size. With the buttons Straight, Curves, Turnouts etc. above it you see only that type.
    4. Place the first curve. Click Curves and then once on a curve with a small radius. It appears on the board. Its free end is orange: that is where the next track goes.
    5. Complete the semicircle. Keep clicking the same curve. Each click attaches a piece to the orange end until a semicircle is formed (with 30° curves that is six pieces; the angle is shown with the track in the library). It is quicker with the Enter key: it attaches the last chosen track again each time.
    6. Attach straights. Click Straight and two or three times on a straight. The line now continues straight ahead.
    7. Build the second half. Place six curves again and then as many straights as before. When the oval closes, the program connects the two ends by itself. The status bar at the bottom then shows Open ends: 0.
    8. Add a siding. Click a straight in the oval and delete it with Del. Two orange ends appear. Click one of them, then a turnout under Turnouts – it is inserted there. Close the gap with a straight; if it does not fit exactly, click Tracks › Close gap. Now click the free end at the branch of the turnout, place two straights and finally a buffer stop under Other.
    9. Check. Press F7 (or Home › Collision check). The list reports whether tracks touch, project over the edge or ends are open. A click on an entry shows the spot. If the list is empty, everything is fine.
    10. Look and save. Click 3D › 3D view and turn the layout with the left mouse button held down. Close the window and save with Ctrl+S; enter a file name. Done – your first layout!
    TipIf the turnout faces the wrong way, press A (change connector) or F (mirror) – this turns the last track placed. If a track lies off to one side, drag it with the mouse to an orange end: it snaps on by itself.

    Moving the view

    • Zoom: mouse wheel, the keys + and − or the zoom bar at the bottom right.
    • Pan: hold the right or middle mouse button and drag – or hold Space and drag with the left mouse button. Alternatively use the tool Home › Pan.
    • Show the whole layout: key 0 or View › Whole layout.

    Rotating the view freely

    The track plan can be rotated on screen to any angle – for example so that the layout lies the way you will later stand in front of it. Only the view is rotated: tracks, dimensions, parts list and printout remain unchanged.

    • With the mouse: hold Ctrl and turn the mouse wheel – in 5° steps, with Shift as well in 1° steps.
    • Via the ribbon: View › Rotate with 15° left, 15° right and Rotate view… for an exact angle.
    • Back: while the view is rotated, a compass at the top right shows the plan's north direction and the angle. A click on it – or View › Straighten – straightens the view again.

    The dimension chains at the baseboard rotate along; their labels always stay readable.

    Design and font

    Under View › Design and font (also in the menu File › Appearance…) you choose the look of the program. The choice is saved and also applies at the next start. After Apply the window is briefly rebuilt; the open plan is kept.

    DesignAppearance
    Logo greenDefault: green and anthracite from the logo, light workspace.
    Classic blueBlue accents, light workspace.
    BlueprintThe plan as a technical drawing: white tracks on a blue background.
    High contrastBlack tracks on a white baseboard – easy to read, even in bright light.
    WorkshopWarm wood tones: baseboard like plywood, accents in copper.

    Seven fonts are available – from the standard font and a narrow technical font to a serif font and a typewriter font. The font size can be set in four steps (Small, Normal, Large, Very large); a preview in the dialog shows the effect immediately.

    Language

Under File › Sprache / Language… you switch it; on Windows the window then restarts and the open plan is kept, on Android the new language applies from the next start of the app. Plans do not depend on the language.

There are also the groups Rotate and Appearance (see above) and About with version and edition. Controls and keyboard shortcuts (F1) shows all mouse and keyboard commands at a glance.

    6Creating a plan and attaching tracks

    This chapter summarises the basic moves and shows all baseboard shapes. A detailed guide for getting started is in chapter 4.

    Creating a new plan

    1. Choose File › New (Ctrl+N).
    2. Choose the gauge.
    3. Choose the shape of the baseboard and enter the dimensions. The preview shows the shape immediately.
    4. Confirm with Create plan.
    5. Then enter the name of the plan in the properties under Plan name.
    Baseboard shapeDimensions and particulars
    Rectanglewidth and depth
    L shapeouter dimensions and leg depth, orientation in 90° steps
    U shapeouter dimensions and leg depth; the two legs may have different lengths
    T and Z shapeouter dimensions, leg depth and orientation
    E and G shapeouter dimensions, leg depth and orientation; the E shape has three legs, the G shape an inward hook
    Ringlayout all round with an open centre, for example for an access hatch in the middle
    Moduleslayout made of individual rectangular modules (chapter 7)

    Shape and dimensions can be changed at any time later: click a free spot of the drawing area or choose Scenery › Baseboard; the properties then show the baseboard dimensions. More about the baseboard, modules and benchwork is in chapter 7.

    Choosing a track system

    In the track library, choose the track system you want to build with under Manufacturer / track system. The library then shows all track pieces of this system with article number and dimensions. You can also mix several systems in one plan.

    Attaching tracks

    1. Click a curved track in the library. It appears in the middle of the baseboard; its free end is marked orange – this is the active track end.
    2. Keep clicking curves. Each new track is attached to the active end until the semicircle is complete.
    3. Add straights and the second semicircle. When the oval closes, TrackMaster Pro connects the ends automatically.
    4. Click the orange end of another track to continue building there, or press E for the next open end.
    5. Insert a turnout, run a parallel track and connect it again with a second turnout.
    TipWith A (switch connector) you lay the most recently placed track with its other connector – this quickly turns a right-hand turnout in the direction of travel into one that receives the track from the other side.

    Checking the plan

    Click Home › Collision check (F7). The list shows overlaps, spacing that is too tight and open ends; a click on an entry shows the spot in the plan (chapter 13).

    Sample plan

    Under File › Load sample plan the layout “Newtown station” opens, with scenery, wiring and signals. It is well suited for trying out all features.

    7Baseboard, modules and benchwork

    The baseboard can be dragged with the mouse or typed in to the millimetre, divided into modules, rotated and given a timber benchwork.

    Drag baseboard and type in dimensions

    Switch on Scenery › Drag baseboard. The baseboard then shows handles:

    • Dragging corners changes width and depth. Baseboards and modules are at least 50 × 50 mm.
    • Round handles on L, U, T, Z, E, G shape and ring: at the inner edge dragging changes the depth of the leg, at the free end its length.
    • Dragging a module moves it together with its tracks and terrain; its edges snap to the neighbouring modules.
    • Clicking without dragging opens the dimensions for typing in: for a module its name, position (X, Y of the top left corner), width and depth, otherwise width, depth and leg depth of the baseboard. A click on a round handle opens Individual legs….

    Esc ends the tool. Every step can be undone with Ctrl+Z.

    Individual legs

    On L, U, T, Z, E, G shape and ring the legs initially share one depth. Under Individual legs… (in the baseboard dimensions or in the baseboard properties) each leg gets its own depth and, where it fits, its own length. Empty fields take the common leg depth or the full length; Reset all restores the initial state.

    Rotate layout

    Scenery › Rotate layout 90° rotates the baseboard together with tracks, terrain and objects by 90° clockwise; rotating four times gives the initial state again. In the dimensions dialog of a module, Rotate 90° rotates only that module with its content. To rotate only the view, see chapter 5; free rotation in all directions is available in the 3D view (chapter 14).

    Layout made of modules

    Under Scenery › Modules… the layout is assembled from rectangular modules. An existing baseboard is split into modules, one per rectangle of the shape. The table shows each module's name, position (X, Y) and size in millimetres.

    CommandEffect
    Add modulenew module to the right of the layout
    Merge selectedjoins the ticked modules into one if together they form a rectangle
    Splitdivides a module vertically (split width) or horizontally (split depth) into two modules – at a position entered in mm or, with an empty field, in the middle. The parts are called “… a” and “… b”.
    Attachplaces a module with its long or short side flush to the right, bottom, left or top of another one; it is rotated by 90° if necessary
    Attach plan as module…places a saved plan as a new module next to the layout, with all its tracks and terrain
    ✕removes the module

    Only Apply changes the plan. Tracks and terrain move with their module. The plan check reports tracks that run across a module joint.

    Height Z (web version): In the column Height Z you set a module higher or lower than the others, e.g. a station module 80 mm above the line or a harbour basin 40 mm lower (negative value). The tracks on the module move along, newly laid tracks lie on its surface, and the 3D view shows the module at its height. Bridge the difference to the neighbouring module with a gradient (Tracks › Gradient…); until then the plan check shows the transition as an open track end. The size dialog of a single module (Scenery › Drag baseboard, click a module) also has the Height Z field. The column Thickness or the field Baseboard thickness sets the thickness of the module board (empty = default); in the 3D view a raised module appears as a board of this thickness.

    Cutting tracks at the module edge (web version). Right-click a track that runs across a module edge and choose Cut at module edge. The program splits the track exactly at the edge into two flex track pieces. Position, height and gradient stay the same; feeders and isolating joints move along. The same function is available under Scenery › Cut at module edge: it splits the selected tracks, or all tracks across module edges if nothing is selected. Turnouts, crossings and turntables cannot be split; they must lie entirely on one module.

    Timber benchwork

    Scenery › Benchwork… plans the timber frame under the baseboard: aprons all round and cross members under each baseboard part, legs in the corners and along the long sides, and if you wish foot braces, levelling feet and a top board.

    SettingMeaning
    Height of board top above floorworking height of the layout
    Top thicknessthickness of the top board; 0 gives an open grid for rib construction
    Frame battenthickness and height of aprons and cross members; cross members at most every … mm
    Legssquare timber or L-leg from two battens, width and depth, spacing at most … mm
    Foot braces, levelling feetheight of the foot braces and of the levelling feet (M8 with tee nut)
    Stock length, sheet sizetrade sizes the material list calculates with

    The material list names battens and square timber by trade length, sheets, screws and glue. The cutting list shows each part with cross-section, length and quantity; both can be copied or saved as a CSV file. Parts that are too long and top boards that are too large are reported with a hint on how to join them. The 3D view shows the benchwork under the baseboard. Remove in the dialog takes it out of the plan again.

    8Laying and editing track

    Track library

    • Choose the gauge at the top (Z, N, TT, H0, 0, G and further gauges in the list).
    • Choose the track system in the drop-down list.
    • Search by article number or name; Tracks › Search track jumps straight into the search field.
    • Filter by All, Straight, Curves, Turnouts, Crossings and Other.
    • Clicking attaches the track to the active end, dragging places it freely on the baseboard; near a free end it snaps on.

    The group Tracks › Lay track offers quick commands for Straight, Curve, Left turnout, Right turnout, Buffer stop and Crossing of the selected system.

    Turntables are available for every gauge and every track system; you find them in the library under the filter Turntables and transfer tables (or search for “turntable”). They are attached like tracks; every track exit is a track end of its own. In the web version Tracks › Turntable attaches the turntable of the selected system directly at the active track end. Where the manufacturer offers no turntable of its own, a matching one of another manufacturer or a generic turntable (“generic”) is provided – its dimensions are preliminary and should be checked against the real thing before building.

    Note on dimensionsThe track geometry follows the catalogue data. A few track systems are stored with provisional dimensions and marked in the library; check these against the real product before building.

    Selecting and moving

    • Click on a track selects it, Shift+click adds more.
    • Dragging on a free area draws a selection rectangle; Ctrl+A selects everything.
    • Dragging moves the selection. If a free end comes close to another one, the whole group snaps on there. A single track snaps onto any free end, even if it lies the wrong way round – it is turned to fit.
    • Arrow keys move by 1 mm, with Shift by 10 mm.
    • Snapping can be switched off in the status bar with Snap.

    When a track is selected, the properties show its usable length as well as position, rotation and height: the length of the track section up to the next turnout, crossing or track end – in other words, how long a train standing there may be. If a longest train is entered in the plan properties, the usable length turns red when the train does not fit.

    Arrange: lock, group, align

    The Home › Arrange group helps when the plan gets larger:

    Parallel track

    For a double-track line or a passing loop, TrackMaster Pro lays a second track at a fixed spacing next to existing tracks:

    1. Select the tracks of the line (drag a rectangle or Shift+click). They should be connected.
    2. Click Tracks › Parallel track….
    3. Enter the track spacing centre to centre – the usual spacing for the gauge is preset – and choose the side: left or right in the direction of travel.

    The new track is laid as flexible track of the selected system; curves automatically get the matching larger or smaller radius. If it ends up on the wrong side, undo it with Ctrl+Z and choose the other side.

    Layout suggestion from your own track

    You have a box full of track but no idea for a layout? The layout suggestion builds small layouts from your quantities – not large layouts, but ideas to get started. For now this feature is only available in the web version.

    1. Set up the baseboard (size and shape) and choose your track system in the track library.
    2. Click Tracks › Layout suggestion….
    3. For each piece, enter how many you have. Empty or 0 means none. If you have already entered your stock in the bill of materials, it is filled in here.
    4. Click Calculate suggestions. After a moment the suggestions appear with a small preview, the number of tracks and the size.
    5. If you like one, click Apply. Ctrl+Z removes it again.

    Depending on your stock, these shapes are offered: oval, oval with siding (or two), oval with passing loop, double oval (two loops one inside the other) and the figure eight with a crossing in the middle. Every suggestion is closed, fits the baseboard and passes the plan check; it never uses more pieces than you entered.

    No suggestion? An oval needs curves for a full circle of one radius (e.g. 12 curves of 30°), and the baseboard must be at least twice the radius deep plus a little margin. Sidings need a turnout, a matching counter curve and a buffer stop. If “Replace existing tracks” is ticked, the suggestion replaces the tracks in the plan; otherwise it is added.

    Flexible track

    Flexible track can be laid in three ways:

    Draw flexible track

    1. Switch on Tracks › Draw flexible track.
    2. Put the mouse on an open track end and drag. The flexible track leaves the track tangentially and bends along.
    3. If you come close to another track end, it snaps on: the flexible track is then laid as a matching double curve that joins both ends without a kink.
    4. Release the mouse button. Length and smallest radius are shown while dragging.

    Reshaping: with the tool switched on, grab a laid flexible track at one end and drag – the other end stays in place.

    Close gap

    Click a track end (active end), select the counterpart and click Tracks › Close gap. TrackMaster Pro connects both ends with a suitably bent flexible track.

    Flexible track with dimensions

    Via Tracks › Flexible track… or the entry “Flexible track…” in the library you create a straight piece of any length or a curve with any radius and angle.

    If a track system has flexible tracks of its own, they are listed individually in the library. The parts list counts flexible track in whole lengths of the respective size, with 5 % waste.

    CautionWhen dragging, watch the smallest radius shown. It should not be smaller than the smallest curve radius your vehicles can negotiate.

    9Heights, levels, helixes and rib construction

    Height of a track

    Every track has a top-of-rail height at its start and a gradient in percent. You enter both in the properties; TrackMaster Pro calculates the height at the other end. With View › Heights the heights at the track ends appear in the plan; higher sections are always drawn above lower ones.

    Gradient over a track sequence

    1. Select the tracks of the ramp (draw a rectangle or Shift+click).
    2. Click Tracks › Gradient….
    3. Enter the start height and choose under Input whether you give the height at the end (the gradient in percent is calculated from the length of the track sequence) or the gradient in percent; reverse the direction if needed. Below the fields you see the ramp length and the gradient or end height.

    The gradient is applied evenly over all selected tracks. If two parallel track sequences are selected – such as the two tracks of a double-track ramp or helix – the inner, shorter track gets exactly the gradient entered; the outer one rises so that it arrives at the same height.

    GradientAssessment
    up to 2 %close to the prototype, suitable for all trains
    2 to 3 %common on model railway layouts
    over 3 %the plan check warns; long trains need a second locomotive
    over 5 %the plan check reports an error

    Height at a joint (web version). For selected tracks the heights at the joints are shown in the plan (↕ in mm); for raised lines also without a selection once you have zoomed in far enough. Double-click a joint – or right-click and choose Height at joint… – to change the height there. Both tracks at the joint get the new height, their other ends stay in place and the gradient adjusts; turnouts and crossings are raised as a whole.

    Height profile

    How high does the line lie at which point, and where is it too steep? Select connected tracks – for example a ramp or the whole line between two stations – and click Tracks › Height profile. The chart shows the height of the top of rail over the distance travelled. Red sections are steeper than recommended. This shows you at a glance where you should make changes.

    Levels

    A layout can have up to 10 levels, for example for the fiddle yard, main level and mountain line. Each level has a name and a base height; every track and every terrain object belongs to exactly one level.

    • Level bar at the bottom of the window: the tick shows or hides the level, a click makes it active, + adds a new level above.
    • Levels dialog (Tracks › Levels… or … in the level bar): add, rename, change base height, remove levels (their tracks go to the level below), show Only this or Show all.
    • New tracks go onto the active level and start at its base height. Selected to active level moves selected tracks; their height changes by the difference in height between the levels.
    • If the base height of a level changes, its tracks move with it. Hills of a level stand on its base height, so the terrain adapts automatically.
    • Hidden levels are missing from the plan and the 3D view, but still count in the parts list and plan check.

    Helix

    A helix overcomes large differences in height on a small area. TrackMaster Pro builds it from curved tracks of the catalogue – round or oval, single-track or double-track.

    1. Click a free track end if the helix should connect there. Without an active end it is created in the middle of the view.
    2. Choose Tracks › Helix….
    3. Choose the shape: Round or Oval – with the oval, straight tracks lie along the two long sides; choose the straight track and the number per side.
    4. Choose single-track or double-track. With double track the second track lies outside: as a catalogue curve with a matching radius or as flexible track with a freely chosen track spacing.
    5. Choose the curved track for the inner track, the number of turns, the height per turn and the direction.
    6. Below the fields, gradient, total height and space required are shown continuously. Click Build helix.

    The height per turn must at least reach the clearance height of the gauge, otherwise no train fits under the next turn; the dialog points this out. Turns lying on top of each other are not connected to each other.

    Under Construction you choose how the track bed is supported: on threaded rods or by rib construction with slotted ribs inside and outside, in whose slots the track bed boards lie. The timber list then contains the ribs with the heights of all slot bottoms instead of the threaded rods.

    Helix timber list PRO

    After building, TrackMaster Pro shows the timber list – everything you need for the helix substructure, with finished cutting dimensions. It can be copied or saved as a CSV file for a spreadsheet.

    PartContent
    Track bed segmentsring segments of plywood with cutting dimensions (length × depth × thickness) and inner and outer radius. 4, 6, 8 or 12 segments per circle, so that none is longer than 1200 mm.
    Straight track bed boardswith the oval, one board per long side and turn
    Joint platesunder every joint, for gluing and screwing
    Base platewith a 50 mm margin all round – or stand the helix directly on the baseboard
    Threaded rods, nuts, washersone rod inside and outside per support point; nuts and large washers above and below each level, so that the height can be fine-tuned
    Notestrack bed width, sheet requirement, gradient inside and outside, clear height and space required

    Allowances

    • Track bed: about 2.7 times the track gauge per track; with double track plus the track spacing.
    • Edge allowance: at least 25 mm per side (for large gauges 1.5 times the track gauge) – room for the threaded rod hole 12 mm from the edge and for a guard rail.
    • Saw allowance: 5 mm, already included in every cutting dimension.
    • Wood thickness: 8 mm for small gauges, 10 mm up to gauge 0, 15 mm for large gauges; base plate at least 12 mm.
    • Threaded rods: M8, M10 for large gauges. The length results from total height, base plate and projection.
    • Sheet requirement: calculated in sheets of 2500 × 1250 mm with 25 % waste. The segments can be nested when cutting.
    TipMark out the segments with a string compass from the centre of the helix and cut them with a jigsaw. Drill the holes for the threaded rods with a template so that all levels lie exactly on top of each other.
    CautionIf the clear height under the track bed is tight, the list shows a warning. Then increase the height per turn or choose thinner wood.

    Cutting template as SVG

    Cutting template as SVG in the timber list window saves each track bed segment, each straight board and each joint plate as a separate SVG file at full size (1:1), together in a ZIP file, with the track centre drawn as a dashed line. Radii, number of segments and the dimensions of boards and plates can be adjusted beforehand. The files are suitable for printing as a template, for a plotter or a CNC router.

    Rib construction of the layout PRO

    In open-grid benchwork the roadbeds lie on ribs that stand on the frame at fixed spacing. Tracks › Rib construction… calculates these ribs from the track heights of the plan.

    • Rib direction: crosswise (parallel to the depth) or lengthwise (parallel to the width), with the maximum spacing in mm.
    • Roadbed width and thickness as well as the top of rail at height 0 above the frame.
    • Each support gets its height and slope from the track heights; next to the supports the top edge falls away at 45°.
    • If one roadbed lies under another, the rib gets a cut-out with the clearance height of the gauge – so the same ribs carry several levels.

    The result shows each rib with position, length, height, supports and cut-outs. It can be copied, saved as CSV and output as SVG: an overview and each rib as a 1:1 template in its own file, together in a ZIP file.

    Roadbed from selection PRO

    For a ramp or a freely routed line, Tracks › Roadbed… calculates the roadbed board under the selected tracks:

    1. Select the tracks, for example a gradient.
    2. Choose Tracks › Roadbed… and enter roadbed width, thickness, maximum part length and joint plate length.
    3. The roadbed follows the track centre and is divided into parts of equal length with joint plates. Save the parts as SVG: each part and each plate as its own file at full size, with the track joints and their heights drawn in.

    Tunnel

    Select tracks and click Tracks › Tunnel. Tunnel tracks are drawn below the other tracks in the plan, get portals in 3D and count as permitted under terrain in the plan check.

    ToolHow it works
    Shape terrainClicks set the corner points of a height area (hill, mountain, plateau). Enter closes the area, Esc cancels. Enter the height in the properties.
    WaterDraw a lake or river as a polygon, as with terrain; the depth is in the properties.
    BuildingDraw a rectangle; width, depth, height and rotation can then be entered exactly.

    How the terrain is shown

    • Hills and mountains rise from the edge of the area with a natural slope up to the height entered. In the track plan, height bands and contour lines show how steep the terrain is, as on a hiking map; every fifth line is bolder. The line spacing depends on the height (for example every 20 mm).
    • In the 3D view gentle slopes are meadow, steeper ones earth and very steep ones rock. The meadow on the baseboard has several shades of green.
    • Water has a shore strip. Trees are deciduous or conifers, shown with shadows in the track plan.
    • Buildings, trees and piers stand on the terrain – a tree on the hill therefore stands on top of the slope.
    • Terrain and levels: a hill stands on the base height of its level and grows with it. In the 3D view with scenery, raised tracks get an embankment sloping down to the ground; over other tracks piers remain, so that a train fits underneath.

    11Room and background image

    Will the layout actually fit into the room? Can the door still open, and can you reach every spot? In the Room tab you draw your room with doors, windows and furniture around the layout. A background image – such as a floor plan or an old track plan – helps you trace.

    Creating a room

    For a rectangular room one dialog is enough:

    1. Measure the room inside, from wall to wall, and note where the layout is to stand.
    2. Click Room › Room….
    3. Enter the inner width, inner depth and wall thickness (all in mm).
    4. Under Inner corner X and Inner corner Y state where the top left corner of the room lies – measured from the top left corner of the baseboard. If the board stands 50 cm from the left wall and 30 cm from the top wall, enter −500 and −300. Negative means: to the left of or above the board.
    5. Click Create. The walls appear in grey around the board.

    If the room has alcoves or slanted walls, draw it with Room › Draw room: click all corners of the inner face of the wall one after the other. A double-click or Enter closes the room, Esc cancels. You select a room by clicking a wall; the properties show its name, wall thickness and floor area in m².

    Doors, windows and furniture

    The Room › Room elements group contains Door, Window, Column, Radiator and Furniture. Choose the element, click the spot in the plan or drag a rectangle of the size you want. Then enter width, depth, height and rotation exactly in the properties; the type can also be changed there afterwards. For a door the plan shows the swing area – the space the door leaf needs when opening. Esc ends the tool.

    What the plan check reports in the room

    FindingMeaning
    Board over the wallError: the baseboard projects beyond a wall – it will not fit into the room like this.
    Door cannot openError: the swing area of a door lies under the board.
    Room element under the boardWarning: a column, radiator or furniture stands under or against the board. Windows may lie under the board.
    Aisle too narrowNote: less than 60 cm remains between board and wall. That much space is comfortable for building and operating. If the board stands right against the wall (up to 15 cm away), this is treated as intended and not reported.
    TipRooms and room elements are included in the DXF export (layer RAUM) – handy if you continue planning in a CAD program.

    Loading a background image

    An image under the plan helps you rebuild an existing track plan from a book or plan the layout to a floor plan.

    1. Click Room › Load image… and choose a photo or scan (PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP). Very large images are reduced when loading so that the plan stays manageable.
    2. The image now lies semi-transparent under the plan. Under Position and opacity… you set where the top left corner lies (Left, Top in mm), how wide the image is and how strongly it appears (Opacity in %). There you can also replace it with another image or remove it.
    3. Show image hides it and shows it again. The image is saved with the plan.

    Scaling the image to a measurement

    To give the image exactly the right scale, measure a distance on it whose real length you know – such as a wall of the room or a track:

    1. Switch on Home › Dimension and click the start and end of the known distance in the image.
    2. Click the new dimension line so that it is selected.
    3. Click Room › Scale to measurement…. The dialog tells you how long the line currently is in the image. Enter the real length in mm and click Scale.

    The image is enlarged or reduced to fit. You can then delete the dimension line and lay your tracks exactly on the image.

    12Wiring, signals and digital

    Feeders and isolating joints

    • Wiring › Feeder: a click on a track places a connection. In the properties you assign it to a circuit or booster.
    • Wiring › Isolating joint: a click on a track joint places insulated rail joiners.
    • Wiring › Show circuits colours every power section in a colour of its own.
    • A second click with the same tool on an existing feeder or signal removes it again; delete selected objects with Del.

    Checking the wiring

    Wiring › Check wiring shows sections without a feeder, reverse loops and addresses used twice. TrackMaster Pro detects reverse loops automatically in two-rail systems – there a reverse loop circuit with isolating joints is required. For centre-rail track systems this check is omitted, because reverse loops need no special circuit there.

    Signals

    Under Wiring › Place signal you choose the type of signal and then click a track end. The signals are shown as light signals of the German H/V signal system with a prototypical signal head – in the plan, in the 3D view and in the selection. Each shows its stop aspect; dark lamps can be seen in their real positions.

    SignalStop aspectSignal head
    Main signalHp 0 – stopgreen at the top, red in the middle, yellow at the bottom (green + yellow = Hp 2, proceed slowly); red and white mast plate
    Exit signalHp 00 – stoptwo red lamps side by side, plus green, yellow and two white lamps for Sh 1 (shunting permitted)
    Block signalHp 0 – stopgreen at the top, red at the bottom
    Distant signalVr 0 – expect stoptwo yellow lamps rising to the right, two green (Vr 1), yellow + green (Vr 2)
    Shunting signalSh 0 – stop, no movementdwarf signal: two red lamps horizontally, two white rising to the right (Sh 1)

    Decoders and addresses

    Wiring › Decoders lists all turnouts and signals with their digital addresses. Addresses can be entered individually or assigned automatically; conflicts are marked. Turnouts with two motors (three-way turnout, double slip) use two addresses.

    Detectors

    Detectors tell a digital control system where a train is at the moment. Choose Wiring › Detector and then the type:

    • Occupancy detector – reports an occupied track from the current drawn by the vehicles.
    • Contact track – reports when a train passes a particular spot.
    • Reed contact – triggered by a magnet under the vehicle.

    Then click the tracks. The contacts are numbered consecutively – 16 per feedback module. Clicking an existing detector removes it. Select a detector to change its name, type and contact number in the properties. The detectors appear in the wiring plan with module and input, in the parts list together with the number of feedback modules needed, and in the export for control software.

    Wiring plan

    Wiring › Wiring plan summarises the wiring: power sections with their feeders, all isolating joints, the detectors and the decoder assignment. It can be saved as a text file in the Pro edition.

    Routes

    Wiring › Routes finds all paths from one signal to the next signal in the direction of travel, to a buffer stop or to an open track end. Each route shows the turnouts with their position (straight, diverging, left or right), the length and the decoder addresses. Routes that need the same turnouts set differently are marked as a conflict.

    Export for control software PRO

    Wiring › Control export saves turnouts, signals, addresses, power sections and routes as a JSON file. From the routes window they can also be saved as text or CSV. This lets you take your planning into a layout control program without typing addresses twice.

    15Saving, parts list, printing and export

    Saving small plans and opening work in both editions; the parts list, printing, the exports and saving large plans belong to the Pro edition.

    Saving and opening

    In the Basic edition, plans with up to 50 tracks can be saved; the Pro edition saves larger plans.

Plan files are interchangeable between Windows and Android and do not depend on the interface language. A plan file can also be opened with “Open with” or as a start parameter: TrackMasterPro.exe MyLayout.trackmaster.json.

    Parts list PRO

    Home › Parts list counts all tracks by article number, with description and quantity, and adds the accessories: turnout motors, signals, feeders, insulated rail joiners, decoders, detectors and feedback modules. Flexible track is counted in whole lengths.

    What does the layout cost?

    1. In the In stock column, enter how many pieces you already own. The To buy column shows what is still missing.
    2. In the Price column, enter the unit price. Below, the costs are updated as you type: Still to buy (only what is missing), All bought new and how many articles are still without price.
    3. Click Close – prices and stock are saved with the plan and are there again the next time you open it.

    Importing a price list: instead of typing in all prices, use Import price list… to read a text or CSV file, for example from a spreadsheet. Each line contains the article number (or the description) at the front and the price after it, separated by semicolon, tab or comma:

    10101; Straight 188 mm; 2.49
10202; Curve R2 30°; 2.79

    Articles that do not occur in the plan are skipped. With Save as CSV… you take the whole list including stock and prices into a spreadsheet – as a shopping list for your dealer.

    Printing PRO

    File › Print… (Ctrl+P) offers two kinds:

    • Overview: the plan with title, baseboard and summary, reduced to one page.
    • Building template 1:1: the plan at full size on several pages. Neighbouring pages overlap by a glue strip; registration marks and the page labels (A1, A2, B1 …) help when gluing them together. Lay the template on the baseboard and transfer the track layout. Set the actual size (100 %) in the print dialog and measure the 100 mm test dimension on each page.

    Via the Windows print dialog you can also print to a PDF file.

    Export PRO

    • SVG at 1:1 scale (File › Export as SVG (1:1)…): a vector graphic at full size, for example as a template for a plotter or for printing on several pages.
    • PNG image (File › Export as PNG image…): the whole plan as a picture, about 4000 pixels wide – for showing by e-mail, in a forum or for printing.
    • DXF drawing (File › Export as DXF (CAD)…): the plan at 1:1 in millimetres for CAD programs, CNC routers and laser cutters. The parts lie on separate layers: PLATTE (baseboard), GLEISACHSE (track centre line), SCHIENEN (rails), PRELLBOCK (buffer stops), GELAENDE (scenery), RAUM (room) and TEXT.
    • Parts list as CSV (File › Parts list as CSV).
    • Timber list as CSV from the timber list window.
    • Wiring plan as a text file from the wiring plan window.
    • Cutting templates as SVG for helixes, ribs and roadbeds: one file per part at full size, together in a ZIP file.
    • Ribs as CSV and the material and cutting list of the benchwork as CSV (the benchwork belongs to the Basic edition).
    • Export for control software (chapter 12).

    18Questions and solutions

    A track does not snap on.

    Check whether Snap is switched on in the status bar. Tracks only snap onto free ends; if the end is already connected, choose the next open end with E.

    An oval does not quite close.

    Small gaps occur when straights of different lengths are mixed. Close the gap with Tracks › Close gap or draw a flexible track.

    A note about the Pro edition appears when saving.

    The Basic edition saves plans with up to 50 tracks. The Pro edition saves larger plans. Install your licence under File › Licence (chapter 3).

    The licence is reported as invalid.

    The file must not be changed – not even by opening and saving it in a text editor. If the licence is bound to a computer, it applies only there; if an expiry date is entered, only until that day. Contact the publisher with your machine ID.

    The licence is valid but “not activated on this device”.

    The licence already runs on two other devices, or the device had no internet connection for 14 days. Release another device or connect to the internet and click Activate now in the licence dialog (section Online activation).

    Windows reports “Windows protected your PC” during installation.

    Click More info and Run anyway. The message appears for programs that Windows has not yet seen often.

    Android does not install the app.

    Allow the app you use to open the file (for example “Files” or the browser) to install unknown apps. If an older version with a different signature was already installed, uninstall it first.

    Tracks have suddenly disappeared.

    Their level is probably hidden. Tick it again in the level bar at the bottom or choose Show all in the Levels dialog. If Show scenery is off, hills, water, buildings and trees are missing as well.

    A track cannot be moved, rotated or deleted.

    It is locked. Select it and click Home › Lock, or untick locked in the properties.

    One click suddenly selects many tracks.

    The tracks are grouped. Home › Ungroup separates them again.

    The background image does not match my dimensions.

    Draw a line over a known distance in the image with Home › Dimension, select it and call Room › Scale to measurement… (chapter 11).

    The plan check says the door cannot open.

    The swing area of the door lies under the board. Move the door, rotate it in the properties or make the board smaller at that spot.

    How much does my layout cost?

    Open the parts list (Pro), enter stock and prices or import a price list. The total is shown at the bottom of the list (chapter 15).

    I have changed too much.

    Home › History… shows the last 20 steps; a click jumps to an earlier state, and forward again.

    The 3D view stays black or stutters.

    Update the graphics driver. For very large layouts it helps to place trees and buildings sparingly at first.

    The interface appears in the wrong language.

    Choose File › Sprache / Language… and select the language. The menu item is labelled in both languages so it can always be found.

    Where is my data?

    Plans are where you saved them. Licence, settings and the automatic backup are in %APPDATA%\TrackMaster Pro. The program sends no data to third parties; at start-up it only fetches the current version number from the download page.

    19Glossary

    TermMeaning
    Active track endThe free end marked in orange, where the next track is attached.
    Apron, cross memberOuter frame and cross bracing of the benchwork on which the top board or the ribs rest.
    Background imageImage under the plan, such as a floor plan or a track plan from a book, for tracing.
    BaseboardSurface on which the layout is built, usually a wooden board on benchwork.
    Buffer stopEnd piece of a track where the train stops.
    Clear heightFree space between the top of rail and the underside of the track bed above.
    Clearance heightMinimum height between two tracks lying on top of each other so that vehicles fit underneath; depends on the gauge.
    ConnectorOne end of a track piece; a turnout has three, a crossing four connectors.
    CSV fileSimple table file that any spreadsheet can open.
    CurveCurved track piece with a fixed radius and angle, for example 30°. Twelve 30° curves make a circle.
    DetectorTells the digital control whether a track is occupied or a train passes a spot.
    DXF fileDrawing format for CAD programs, CNC routers and laser cutters.
    Edge allowanceAddition to the track bed width for fixing and a guard rail.
    FeederPower connection of a track section to a transformer, command station or booster.
    Flexible trackTrack supplied in lengths that can be bent to any radius and cut to length.
    GaugeSize of the model railway, for example H0, N or Z; sets track width and scale.
    GradientHow steeply a track climbs, in per cent: 2 % means 2 cm of height over 1 m of track.
    GroupTracks that are combined and are selected and moved together with one click.
    HelixSpiral climbing line for overcoming large differences in height on a small area.
    Isolating jointInsulated rail joint that separates two power sections.
    Layout suggestionSmall layout the program builds from the quantities of track you entered (web version).
    LevelGroup of tracks and terrain objects with a common base height that can be shown and hidden together.
    ModuleRectangular part of a layout with its own dimensions that can be combined with others into a layout.
    Open track endEnd of a track piece without a connection; marked orange in the plan. The plan check reports open ends without a buffer stop.
    OvalSimplest layout shape: two semicircles joined by straights. The train runs round endlessly.
    Parallel trackSecond track at a constant spacing next to a line.
    PropertiesArea on the right of the window; shows and changes the values of the selected track or object.
    RadiusDistance from the centre of the circle to the middle of the track of a curve; the smaller, the tighter.
    Reverse loopTrack arrangement in which a train returns to the line in the opposite direction; in two-rail systems this creates a short circuit that needs a special circuit.
    RibUpright board on the frame on which the roadbed lies in open-grid benchwork.
    RibbonBar at the top of the window with all commands, arranged in tabs.
    RoadbedBoard under the tracks that carries them at the desired height.
    RoutePath from one signal to the next with the turnout positions required.
    Saw allowanceAddition to the cutting dimension for the saw cut and finishing.
    ScaleRatio between model and prototype; in H0, 1 cm on the model is 87 cm in reality (1:87).
    SidingTrack that ends at a buffer stop, where trains or wagons are parked.
    Stop aspectSignal indication that means “stop” (Hp 0, Hp 00, Vr 0, Sh 0).
    TabOne of the headings in the ribbon (Home, Tracks, Scenery, Room …); a click shows its commands.
    Top of railReference height of all height values in the plan.
    Track systemTrack range from one manufacturer; its pieces fit together exactly.
    TurnoutTrack piece where a track divides into two (or three) directions.
    Usable lengthLength of a track section between turnouts or up to the buffer stop – the longest train that can stand there.
